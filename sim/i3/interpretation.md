## Interpretation (surrogate learner only; not evidence about patients)

- Hypothesis "CP gives few training falls AND high no-robot retention" holds only partly: with lambda=0.5, CP had 2-5 training falls (B0: 90-121) and the best retention for mild (+19.7 +/- 9.3 pp vs B0) and moderate (+7.2 +/- 6.1 pp) learners; for severe learners nobody learned anything (all retention equals untrained, 47-48%).
- B1 (lock) and B2 (spring) also avoid falls but give NO retention gain over the untrained policy (and -6 pp vs B0 for mild); with lambda=0 their learners receive no error signal at all (no falls, identical reward), so they learn nothing, i.e. 'dependence' here means 'assistance removes the learning signal'.
- The effect depends on lambda: with error-only reward (lambda=0), B0 retains better than CP for mild learners (79 vs 65%, paired -14.1 +/- 12.8 pp), so CP's advantage appears only when the effort penalty is on (the penalty drags the unassisted B0 learner toward short, under-placed steps; the mechanism for CP's gain was not isolated).
- The scalar Emken-style learner agrees only qualitatively: always-on/lock assistance destroys retention (39%), CP preserves most of it (84/70/40% mild/mod/severe) with zero falls, but unassisted B0 still retains best (90/86/79%) at the price of 35-65 falls.
- Caveats: 12 seeds/cell, 240 training pushes, 4-parameter policy, hand-set learner hyperparameters/reward and dose (0.2-0.6 m/s; 0.3-0.8 gave a plateau where nothing was learnable), single run of each design; results are properties of this learner and reward, not of patients.
